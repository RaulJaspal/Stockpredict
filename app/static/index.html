    Pick a ticker to analyse

    StockPredict blends two years of price history, a per-ticker statistical model, and live headline sentiment from BBC News, Sky News, The Guardian, CNBC and MarketWatch — including politics — into one honest probability for the next five trading sessions.

    How predictions are made (and what they can't do)
    1. Drift anchor. The probability starts from the ticker's own historical 5-session up-rate — in a 10-year walk-forward test (7,278 predictions, 15 tickers, strict no-lookahead) this drift was the strongest signal in price data, so everything else is a tilt around it.
    2. Price tilts. A logistic-regression model trained on that ticker's history and a composite technical read nudge the anchor with small, backtest-validated weights (0.15 and 0.10 — larger weights measurably subtracted accuracy). The most recent 60 sessions are held out to grade this exact blend against an always-up baseline, and that hit-rate ships with every prediction.
    3. News & politics. Live headlines from the official feeds of BBC News, Sky News, The Guardian, CNBC and MarketWatch, plus the company's own news wire, are scored with a finance-tuned sentiment model and weighted by recency (company news half-life 48 h, macro 24 h). News can't be backtested without a historical archive, so it deliberately gets modest weight.
    4. Confidence. Tiers are calibrated on the walk-forward test. Pooled across horizons, "high" calls (≥10 points from a coin flip) were right ~67% of the time vs ~54% for "low" — but that separation comes mostly from month/year-scale drift; at the 5-session horizon every tier sits in the mid-50s. Read "high" as "the evidence lines up", never as a sure thing. Realised volatility gives the expected 5-day price range.

    What this can't do: short-horizon stock moves are mostly noise, and a well-calibrated tool will often — correctly — read close to 50%. The walk-forward test showed no strategy here reliably beats simply knowing that markets drift up; what this app adds is calibration, context and honesty about exactly that. Treat every output as a research signal to investigate, never as a reason on its own to trade.
